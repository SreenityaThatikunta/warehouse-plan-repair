\begin{abstract}
Robots in a grid warehouse carry items from pickup cells to delivery stations along
collision-free paths from prioritized Space-Time A* (ST-A*). While they execute
these paths, robots break down, cells become blocked and robots receive urgent
tasks. We repair the joint plan \emph{locally}. Affected robots negotiate over a
range-limited message bus with the smallest useful set of neighbours, and the
global planner is never re-run. Over 1{,}700 simulated runs (5--50 robots,
0--15\% dynamic obstacle density, 10 seeds each) there were no collisions, and every
task was completed by every repair strategy except one gridlocked run of the
no-negotiation ablation. Local repair changed 5--7$\times$ fewer plans per
disruption than replanning from scratch, at equal or lower total time. A stress study
then identifies the settings in which robots \emph{fail} to finish their tasks:
disruptions that cut goals off, gridlock without negotiation, and emergency deadlines
that become infeasible in dense fleets.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}
A team of $n$ robots moves on a 4-connected grid. In each time step a robot moves to a
neighbouring free cell or waits. Two robots may not occupy the same cell at the same
time (vertex conflict) or swap cells (edge conflict). Each robot has a list of tasks
(go to a pickup cell, then to a delivery station) and returns to its own dock after the
last one. During execution three kinds of disruption occur:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{breakdown}: a robot stops, for 5--20 steps or permanently;
  \item \textbf{blockage}: a free cell becomes an obstacle, for 10--40 steps or permanently;
  \item \textbf{emergency task}: a robot receives a pickup--delivery task that goes before its others.
\end{enumerate}
The assignment requires that disrupted robots repair their plans by communicating with
nearby robots, that global planning is never re-run from scratch, and that as few
robots as possible change their plans. We measure the total time to complete all tasks
(sum of costs, SoC), the number of robots whose plans change per disruption, and how
both scale with the number of robots and the density of dynamic obstacles.

\section{Environment}
\paragraph{Map.} A Kiva-style warehouse of $18\times33$ cells (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}):
$5\times5$ shelf blocks of $2\times5$ cells separated by one-cell aisles, with a free
perimeter corridor. It has 344 free cells, 150 pickup cells (next to a shelf), 14 delivery
stations on the side walls, and 58 private docks on the top and bottom rows. Docks are
dead-end bays, so robots never drive through each other's docks.
\paragraph{Tasks.} Each robot gets 3 random tasks, ordered greedily by nearest pickup.
Picking up and dropping off take no extra time.
\paragraph{Disruptions.} Events are generated up front from the seed, so every strategy
faces the same schedule. They happen in the first 5--70\% of the undisrupted makespan.
The \emph{dynamic obstacle density} $\rho$ is the number of blockage events divided by
the number of free cells. By default a generator filter keeps every instance solvable:
blockages never disconnect the map or land on a goal, and emergency cells are reachable.
Section~\ref{sec:failures} switches this filter off.
\paragraph{Communication.} Robots within Manhattan distance $\R=6$ can exchange messages
(ALERT, STATUS, REQUEST, PROPOSE, COMMIT, ACCEPT, REJECT, UPDATE). Messages are delivered
within the time step, and every message is counted.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Initial plan: prioritized Space-Time A*}
Robots are planned one at a time, longest task chain first. Each path is written into a
space-time reservation table (vertex and edge reservations, plus a parking reservation at
the dock from arrival onwards). ST-A* searches states $(x,y,g,t)$, where $g$ is the index
of the next goal, so a robot's whole pickup$\to$delivery$\to\dots\to$dock chain is one
search. The heuristic is the exact static distance through the remaining goals. If an
attempt fails, the order is randomly restarted.

\subsection{Local repair}
When a disruption happens at time $t$, the robots whose reservations it invalidates are
queued. Each queued robot $a$ is repaired with the tiers below, cheapest in plans changed
first. Let $\ell^\ast$ be $a$'s robot-free (ideal) path, $p_{\text{solo}}$ its best path
with every other plan fixed, and $d(p)=|p|-|\ell^\ast|$ the delay. A coalition $S$ of
neighbours that replan jointly with $a$ (with $a$ planned first) is scored
\begin{equation}
  \text{score}(S)= \underbrace{d(p_a)+\textstyle\sum_{m\in S}\Delta_m}_{\text{extra steps}}
  + \lambda\,|S| + \text{DL}(p_a), \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_m$ is member $m$'s added completion time, $\lambda=3$ is the price of
changing one more robot's plan, and $\text{DL}$ adds a penalty of 10 if the plan makes an
emergency delivery miss its deadline. A coalition is accepted only if its score is below
that of going solo.

\begin{lstlisting}
Repair(a, t):
  $\ell^\ast$ $\leftarrow$ ST-A*(a, ignoring other robots);   $p_{\text{solo}}$ $\leftarrow$ ST-A*(a, others fixed)
  if $d(p_{\text{solo}})\le\delta$ and no deadline is lost then commit $p_{\text{solo}}$; return      // Tier 1
  for each radius $r$ in ($\R$, $2\R$ [, wider ring if a has been holding]) do              // Tiers 2-3
      ALERT robots within $r$;  candidates $\leftarrow$ movable robots whose plans block $\ell^\ast$
      for $k$ = 1 .. max coalition size do
          for each coalition $S$ of $k$ candidates do
              REQUEST/PROPOSE: replan a first, then each $m\in S$;
                  if m fails or $\Delta_m>\delta$: m recruits its own blockers (chain, $\le$2 hops)
          if best score < score(solo) then COMMIT best; REJECT the rest; return
  if a is urgent (emergency, or no solo path) then                                  // Tier 3b
      group $\leftarrow$ movable robots within $2\R$ crossing $\ell^\ast$ ($\le$ 8)
      prioritized ST-A* for a + group; accept if score < score(solo)
  if $p_{\text{solo}}$ exists then commit $p_{\text{solo}}$                       // long detour
  else hold position, re-queue robots planned through a's cell, retry next step  // Tier 4
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{Design details.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Smallest coalition first.} Only robots that actually block $a$'s ideal
        path are candidates (at most 4). Sizes 1, 2, 3 are tried in order, and the search
        stops at the first size whose best coalition beats going solo. A size is skipped
        when $\lambda|S|$ alone already exceeds the solo delay.
  \item \textbf{Chains.} A member $m$ that cannot dodge alone recruits up to 2 robots in
        its own range whose plans block its \emph{relaxed} path (its best path if movable
        neighbours made way), at most 2 hops and 6 robots per tree. The whole tree is
        scored by Eq.~\eqref{eq:score}, so a chain is chosen only if it beats both going
        solo and every flat coalition.
  \item \textbf{Tier 3b} is a bounded group replan around the stuck robot, not a global
        one. It is restricted to urgent robots because applying it to every stuck robot
        changed 0.6 more plans per disruption at 50 robots.
  \item \textbf{Emergencies.} At the event, the deadline is the robot-free delivery time
        plus 3 steps. Emergency robots negotiate for any delay ($\delta=0$), may form
        coalitions of 4, and are never asked to move for others.
  \item \textbf{Permanent breakdown.} The broken robot's cell becomes an obstacle. Its
        unfinished tasks are auctioned to robots in range (lowest marginal cost wins), and a
        carried item is handed off at a reachable neighbouring cell.
  \item \textbf{Expanding ring.} A robot that has held for 10 steps doubles its search
        radius every 10 steps. This fixed a deadlock between two holding robots 16 cells
        apart (beyond $2\R$), each needing the other's cell.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Strategies compared}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{local}: the method above (v2).
  \item \texttt{local-flat}: our first version (flat coalitions only, no Tier 3b, no deadline term).
  \item \texttt{local-nochain}: v2 without chains (robots sweep only).
  \item \texttt{solo}: Tier 1 only, an ablation with no negotiation.
  \item \texttt{full}: re-runs prioritized ST-A* for all live robots from the current state
        after every disruption. This is the reference that the assignment forbids for
        repair; it shows what staying local costs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental setup}
Every configuration was run for 10 seeds with every strategy on identical instances and
event schedules. The defaults are 3 tasks per robot, 3\% dynamic obstacle density (20\%
of blockages permanent), 2 breakdowns (30\% permanent) and 2 emergencies per run.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Robots}: 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50.
  \item \textbf{Density}: $\rho$ = 0, 2, 5, 10, 15\% with 20 robots.
  \item \textbf{Single disruption}: exactly one disruption of each type (temporary or
        permanent blockage or breakdown, emergency) with 10--50 robots. This isolates the
        number of plans changed to handle one disruption.
  \item \textbf{Alter penalty}: $\lambda$ = 0, 1, 3, 6, 12 at 20 and 40 robots.
  \item \textbf{Stress}: settings built to make robots fail (Section~\ref{sec:failures}).
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Altered} counts the robots whose future path after the repair differs from the
plan just before the disruption, including the disrupted robot and all cascades.
\textbf{Direct} counts those the disruption itself invalidated. \textbf{Collateral} is
altered minus direct. Every executed step is checked for vertex, edge and obstacle
collisions, and the test suite (27 tests) re-validates all future plans after every
disruption in a strict mode that forbids reservation overwrites.

\section{Results}
All 1{,}700 runs of the four main sweeps had zero collisions. Every task was completed
in every run, except one \texttt{solo} run discussed in Section~\ref{sec:failures}.

\paragraph{Plans changed.} Local repair changes about one robot beyond those the
disruption hits directly (collateral 0.3--1.4). Full replanning changes 9--39 robots per
disruption, nearly the whole fleet, at no better total time: at 50 robots its SoC is
\emph{worse} (7201 vs 6958), because prioritized planning from scratch reshuffles
priorities and delays robots that were not affected.
\paragraph{Total time.} \texttt{local} has the lowest or near-lowest SoC at every size.
Negotiation pays off as the fleet grows: \texttt{solo} needs 131 more steps than
\texttt{local} at 50 robots, and its overhead rises fastest.
\paragraph{v2 vs v1.} Compared with \texttt{local-flat}, v2 lowers SoC by 7--19 steps at
30--50 robots and raises the emergency on-time rate (65\% vs 45\% at 20 robots, 35\% vs
15\% at 50), changing about as many plans (+0.13 at 50 robots, fewer elsewhere). The
cost is roughly twice the repair time. The \texttt{local-nochain} ablation is within
noise of v2 at half the repair time: chains solve cases flat coalitions cannot (a
covered test case), but such cases are rare in these sweeps.
\paragraph{Scaling.} Repair time grows with fleet size, from milliseconds at 10 robots to
about 2.3\,s per disruption at 50 (Python, single core), mostly spent in ST-A* searches
inside negotiation.

\paragraph{Single disruption.} A temporary breakdown changes 2.2 plans (exactly the robots
hit directly) and a temporary blockage 3.1. Permanent events change more because their
effects last: a permanent breakdown changes 13.5 plans, most of them direct (the robots
that would pass the dead robot plus the winners of its tasks). Full replanning changes
22--29 robots for any single event. A solo replan (Tier 1) settles 94\% of robot repairs at 5
robots but only 57\% at 50: as the fleet grows, negotiation (Tiers 2--3) and Tier 3b take
over a larger share. Holds stay below 0.3\%.

\paragraph{Alter penalty.} Figure~\ref{fig:lambda} shows the trade-off set by $\lambda$:
$\lambda=0$ minimizes time but changes the most plans, and $\lambda=12$ the opposite. At 40
robots the default $\lambda=3$ for v2 is better than v1 at every $\lambda$ on both axes.

\section{Limitations and future work}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Prioritized planning is incomplete.} Neither the initial planner nor the
        joint replans inside negotiation guarantee a solution when one exists. Holds and the
        expanding ring recover in practice, but not provably.
  \item \textbf{Robots never give up a task.} A robot whose goal is permanently cut off
        holds forever. It should detect this, report it, and release or re-auction the task.
  \item \textbf{Emergency deadlines are soft.} The deadline term changes which repair is
        preferred, but it cannot preempt robots already in the way. In dense fleets most
        deadlines (ideal + 3 steps) are missed.
  \item \textbf{Repair time} grows to seconds at 50 robots in Python. Caching searches,
        bounding chains (they add about 2$\times$ for little gain) or a compiled ST-A* would help.
  \item \textbf{Idealized communication}: instant, lossless messages within range. Delays
        and dropped messages are not modelled.
\end{itemize}
